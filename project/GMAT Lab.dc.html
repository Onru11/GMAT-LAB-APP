Percents · Lesson 3

Two changes in a row

The rule
Percent changes multiply. They never add.

Turn each change into a multiplier, then multiply the multipliers.

New = Old × (1 + a) × (1 + b)
A rise of 20% is ×1.2. A fall of 15% is ×0.85.
Worked example
A price rises 10%, then rises another 10%. What is the total change?
{{ el.n }}{{ el.t }}
The second 10% is taken on 110, not 100. That extra 1% is where the points hide.
Common trap
+20% then −20% is not 0%
The second change acts on a new, bigger base. 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96, so you end 4% down. Answer choice “no change” is there to catch you.
